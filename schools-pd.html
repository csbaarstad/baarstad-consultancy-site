▸ How we think about it

Most AI training stops at the bottom of the ladder.

Teachers can already ask a chatbot for a worksheet. That helps for a week. The teachers who get real time back are the ones who climb higher, directing AI to fit their own classroom and building tools they reuse all year. Here is the climb, and where the work actually pays off.

1
Search Replacement
Using AI like a faster Google. A quick answer, a quick explanation. Almost everyone starts here.
2
Generator
Asking for a finished thing and taking the draft. A rubric, a quiz, an email. Useful, but generic, and you start over next week.
Where we focus
3
Director
Steering AI with your standards, your students, and your voice, then editing until it fits. You stay the author. The first rung that saves real time, and where most teachers need a hand.
4
Super-User
Building a reusable assistant that does your recurring work, in your format, once, for the whole year. You leave a session with one already built.
5
Builder
Turning a recurring pain into a tool your whole team uses. For most teachers, this is the rung we build with you.

Our work lives on rungs three and four. The difference between using AI and putting it to work.

▸ What it's for

Three tracks, one practice: the teacher's day, the student's thinking, and the district's policy.

Three audiences, one body of work, built by someone who's done all three. Most schools start with a single faculty session and grow from there.

▸ Track 01

For Teachers

Cutting the workload.
The teacher's own day.
01
The daily writing

The parent emails and admin messages that pile up by Friday. You tell it what you need, it gives you a draft, and you edit and send. You stay the author.

02
The prep

Planning, rubrics, differentiation, the paperwork. The Sunday-night block, cut down to size.

03
Done safely

All of it without putting a student's name in a chatbot. For elementary staff, this track is most of the value.

▸ Track 02

For Teachers Teaching Students

The calculator shift.
Where the real change is.
01
The calculator shift

A calculator freed us to do the real math. AI does that for writing and reasoning: once the mechanics are handled, the work climbs to analysis, argument, and depth.

02
Teaching the skill

Students learn to prompt, push back, and iterate, and to judge whether what comes back is any good. Judging the output is the skill that actually transfers.

03
The integrity question

An honest answer instead of a detector arms race. Work designed to hold up, and a calm stance on what counts as a student's own.

▸ Track 03

For School and District Leadership

Policy, not panic.
A stance you own.
01
A defensible stance

A staff AI policy and an integrity position you can stand behind at a board meeting, not improvise at one.

02
The data line

The student-data and FERPA principles, framed as a starting point your team owns and runs through your own counsel. Your policy governs.

03
Strategic adoption

Rolling it out across sites without chaos: what to pilot, what to approve, and who owns the decisions.

04
Parents and equity

A straight answer for the families and board members who will ask, the home-access gap included, before it turns into heat at a meeting.
