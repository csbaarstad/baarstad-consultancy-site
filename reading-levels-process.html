What's actually happening

Rung three is not a lucky prompt. It is prework. Before the assistant writes a word, the teacher has loaded the real context: the standard being taught, a clear definition of what each reading band means, an exemplar of the handout so the output matches the room, and the target words with a rule to scaffold them in place instead of simplifying them away.

The ask is precise because the setup is precise. Then comes the part only a teacher can do, which is reading the draft critically and pushing back. In the demo that is catching a level-three question that only tests recall and pushing it to inference. The first draft is never the final one. The directing and the editing are where your expertise lives, and they are what make the result usable instead of generic.

That is the line between the rungs. Rung two takes the first draft. Rung three runs the setup, directs the work, and owns the edit.

A few more rung-three moves
  • 01A rubric built from a dense standard, in the language you actually grade with.
  • 02A unit assessment with leveled questions and a clean answer key, aligned to what you taught.
  • 03A bank of feedback drafts tied to your rubric that you curate, cut, and rewrite.
